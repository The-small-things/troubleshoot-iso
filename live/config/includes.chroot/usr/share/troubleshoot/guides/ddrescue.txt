==========================================================================================
  RESCUE A FAILING DRIVE with GNU ddrescue
==========================================================================================

  Use this when a drive is dying (clicking, very slow, "bad sectors" in the test results)
  and you want a copy of EVERYTHING on it before it gets worse. ddrescue copies the
  readable parts first and comes back for the difficult areas later, so you get as much
  data as possible with the least wear on the failing drive.

  You need a second drive that is AT LEAST AS BIG as the failing one.
  !! Everything on the second (destination) drive will be OVERWRITTEN. !!

  1. List the drives and note their names (sda, sdb, nvme0n1...), sizes and models:

         lsblk -d -o NAME,SIZE,MODEL,TRAN

  2. Copy the failing drive (here sdX) to the healthy one (here sdY). First a quick pass
     that skips the bad areas, then a slower pass that retries them:

         ddrescue -f -n /dev/sdX /dev/sdY rescue.map
         ddrescue -f -r3 /dev/sdX /dev/sdY rescue.map

     Double-check the names! Getting them the wrong way round destroys your data.
     The map file lets you stop (Ctrl+C) and continue later with the same command.
     Keep it on a USB stick (e.g. /media/... ) if you want to resume after a restart.

  3. Afterwards, use the copy instead of the failing drive. To recover individual files
     from it, use PhotoRec or the Files app.

  Not sure? Stop here. A professional data-recovery service can often do more, and every
  extra hour a failing drive runs can make things worse.

  Full manual:  info ddrescue      Close this window:  exit
==========================================================================================
